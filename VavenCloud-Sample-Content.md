# VavenCloud Landing Page Copy

Prepared for Vavensoft · 26 October 2026

## How this page sells

The page has one job: get a founder with a live product to book a free production review. Every section moves them one step closer, and every button leads to the same form.

**Who is reading.** A founder or CTO of a live product, usually at night or straight after something broke. They're tired of chasing three vendors, worried their app depends on one developer, and suspicious of anyone who sounds like a hosting company. They don't want more features. They want to stop worrying.

**The journey down the page:**

1. **Recognition** (hero, problem): "This is exactly my situation."
2. **Relief** (one team, every layer): "Someone would actually own this."
3. **Belief** (comparison, how it works, scope): "They've thought this through, and they're specific."
4. **Safety** (exit promise, proof, founder note): "I can try this without getting trapped."
5. **Action** (FAQ, form): "Booking a call costs me nothing, so why not."

**Voice.** Calm, direct, specific. Short sentences. Numbers over adjectives. We talk like the senior engineer they wish they had, not like a vendor. No hype, no exclamation marks, no "cutting-edge" or "world-class".

**Reference point.** [makandra](https://makandra.de/en/managed-hosting-145) wins enterprise clients with one idea: "We take responsibility for availability, not just for deployment." VavenCloud takes the same stance and goes one step further: we also fix the application, and we serve founders, not just enterprises.

Each section below gives the copy exactly as it should appear, followed by a short note in italics on why it works. The notes are for the team, not the page.

Each card is a story the founder has lived or fears, written in their words, so they feel understood before we've sold anything. The cost strip makes inaction feel expensive, which is what turns "interesting" into "I should book this".*

## How it works

**Section label:** How it works

**Headline:**

> From first call to fully covered, without a single surprise.

**Five steps:**

1. **Free production review, 45 minutes.** A senior engineer learns your product, your business model and how it runs today. No slides, no pitch. Just an honest look at what's working and what's at risk.
2. **Your growth roadmap, within 48 hours.** A one-page written plan: the right setup for your budget today, the signals that mean it's time for the next stage, and what that stage will look like and cost. It's yours to keep, even if you never work with us.
3. **Production audit.** Before we take responsibility, we go through your code, infrastructure and security in detail and give you a written list of risks, ranked. You see exactly what we found and why it matters.
4. **Migration and hardening.** We fix the critical risks, move your product to its new setup and switch over with you at a quiet time for your users. Nothing goes live until you've seen it work.
5. **We own it.** Your engineers are on call 24/7, in your portal and your chat. Every month you get a report: uptime, incidents, changes made and what we recommend next.

**Line under the steps:**

> You get a fixed monthly price before any work starts. No hourly meter, no surprise invoices.

*Why it works: uncertainty kills deals, so a clear sequence lowers the perceived effort of switching. Step 2 gives real value away for free, which builds trust and creates a sense of obligation to come back. Step 3 shows we're careful, not reckless, which is what a nervous founder needs to hear before handing over their product. Step 4's "nothing goes live until you've seen it work" tackles the biggest fear about migration directly.*

## FAQ

**Headline:** Questions founders ask before they hand over production.

The order matters: the most common objection first, price last, after the value is clear.

**Is this just managed hosting with a nicer name?**

> No. Managed hosting looks after the server and stops there. We take responsibility for your whole product: infrastructure, deployments, monitoring, incidents and fixes in your code. When something breaks, we don't send you a ticket number. We send you the fix.

**We already have developers. Why would we need you?**

> Because your developers should be building your product, not waking up for server alerts. We take production off their plate and work alongside them in a shared channel. They stay in control of the code; we make sure it runs.

**We're on AWS. Do we have to move?**

> No. You can run on VavenCloud or keep your own AWS, Azure or Google Cloud account, and we take ownership there. In your review we'll tell you honestly which makes more sense for your budget and growth.

**You didn't write our code. How can you own it?**

> That's exactly why every engagement starts with a production audit. We find the risks first, fix the critical ones with you, and only then take responsibility. We'd rather tell you the uncomfortable truth in week one than discover it at 3am.

**What happens when something breaks at night or at the weekend?**

> Your engineers are on call 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Critical incidents get a response within 1 hour on Managed and within 30 minutes on Partner, and we keep you updated in your portal until it's fixed. Afterwards you get a written explanation of what happened and what we changed so it doesn't happen again.

**What if we want to leave?**

> You can, at any time, under the terms of your agreement. Everything runs on open standards, and we hand over documentation, access, runbooks and a full backup. No lock-in, no hostage data.

**Can you take over an app built by an agency, a freelancer or with AI tools?**

> Yes. It's one of the most common reasons founders come to us. We document how your product runs, close the gaps and give you one team that understands it.

**Where is our data stored, and who can access it?**

> In the region you choose, the United States or Europe. Access is limited to your assigned engineers, and every access is logged.

**How much does it cost?**

> A fixed monthly fee, agreed after your free review, once we understand your product. It's usually a fraction of hiring one senior DevOps engineer, and you get a whole team, the infrastructure and fixes in your code. There's no hourly meter and no surprise invoices.

*Why it works: each answer starts with a direct yes or no, then the proof. That's how you answer objections without sounding defensive. Price comes last, after the value is clear, and is framed against a hire rather than against cheap support. "We send you the fix" and "we'd rather tell you the uncomfortable truth" are memorable lines that sound like a senior engineer, not a brochure.*
